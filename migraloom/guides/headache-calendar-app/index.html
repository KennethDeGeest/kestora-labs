A calendar should show what you recorded—not quietly fill the gaps

Monthly views make frequency easier to scan only when headache days, confirmed symptom-free days and unrecorded days stay distinct.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of MigraLoom · Updated November 11, 2026 · 1 minute read

Quick answerWhat should a headache calendar show?

It should show recorded headache days without treating a missing entry as symptom-free, and let you open the detail behind each marked date.

Useful featuresThree day states30 and 90 daysSources

What a useful headache calendar should do

  • Keep recorded headache days distinct from unrecorded days.
  • Open the episode behind a marked date.
  • Use a consistent personal severity scale.
  • Summarize a chosen date range.
  • Export the underlying record, not only a colored calendar.

Three day states matter

Headache recorded

An episode or headache was explicitly entered.

Symptom-free recorded

You actively confirmed no headache for that day.

Not recorded

The diary contains no reliable information for the day.

Use 30-day and 90-day views for different questions

A 30-day view is useful for recent frequency. A 90-day view adds context without turning the calendar into a diagnosis. Keep the original entries so every summary can be checked.
