A headache log with no daily homework

Add a row when an episode happens. Keep timing, severity and medication notes together without maintaining a full calendar.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of MigraLoom · Updated November 11, 2026 · 1 minute read

Quick answerEvent log or daily diary?

An event log records headache episodes only. A daily diary can also confirm symptom-free days. Choose the level you can maintain and define what blanks mean.

Why these five fields?

Date

Places the episode in the wider history.

Timing

Separates when it began from how long it lasted.

Severity

Keeps one personal scale across entries.

Medication

Records what you took and when.

Symptom or impact

Preserves useful detail for later.

When a simple log works best

Use an event log when remembering episodes matters more than a daily check-in. Choose a monthly diary when confirmed symptom-free days and overall frequency matter.

Editorial note: This guide is general information, not medical advice. It was reviewed against the sources below. A diary can support a healthcare conversation; it cannot determine a diagnosis or treatment.
