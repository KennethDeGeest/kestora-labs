A headache diary you can actually keep

Use a monthly overview for the pattern at a glance, then add episode detail only when more context feels useful.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of MigraLoom · Updated November 11, 2026 · 1 minute read

Quick answerWhat belongs in a headache diary?

Record whether head pain occurred, approximate timing, severity, symptoms, medication taken and useful context. Keep unknown information unknown.

On this page

The smallest useful set of fields

Timing

Date, approximate start and end, or duration.

Severity

One consistent personal scale.

Symptoms

Only what you noticed and want to remember.

Medication

Name, dose and time as recorded—not advice.

Impact

Work, sleep or usual activities affected.

Context

Optional details for a later conversation.

Keep the diary from becoming a burden

  • Choose a minimum entry for difficult days.
  • Record near the event when possible.
  • Treat a blank as “not recorded,” not “nothing happened.”
  • Review questions before an appointment instead of diagnosing a pattern.
  • Keep the raw record so summaries can be checked.

When paper helps—and when an app may help more

Paper is immediate and flexible. An app may be easier for searchable history, date-range summaries or a structured report. The useful format is the one you can maintain honestly.

Editorial note: This guide is general information, not medical advice. It was reviewed against the sources below. A diary can support a healthcare conversation; it cannot determine a diagnosis or treatment.
