Format comparison · Paper, app or hybrid

The best migraine diary is the one that survives your real routine.

Paper is immediate and visible. Apps make history easier to search and summarize. A hybrid can work—but only if it does not create double entry.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of MigraLoom · Updated November 5, 2026

There is no medically superior format

The American Migraine Foundation describes written journals, spreadsheets and smartphone apps as valid ways to record symptoms. Consistency, clarity and usefulness to you and your healthcare conversations matter more than the medium.

Paper, app or hybrid?

Printable diary

Best when you prefer handwriting, want a visible sheet and do not need automatic summaries.

Tracker app

Best when you want searchable history, date-range summaries, reminders or structured export.

Hybrid

Best when paper is easiest during an episode but a digital record is more useful before appointments.

Five decision questions

  • What will be within reach during symptoms?
  • Do you need confirmed symptom-free days or only episode records?
  • Will you review patterns manually or want date-range summaries?
  • How do you want to bring the record to an appointment?
  • Which privacy and backup tradeoffs are acceptable to you?

A hybrid without double entry

Use paper for a minimal timestamp or bedside note, then transfer only the essential details to the primary digital record. Treat the digital entry as a later transcription, not as information captured at the original time. Avoid maintaining two complete histories that can silently disagree.
