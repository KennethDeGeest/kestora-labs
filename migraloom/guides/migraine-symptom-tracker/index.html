MigraLoom guide

Track what happened—not what a pattern must mean

A symptom record preserves timing and context for a healthcare conversation. It should not turn a repeated detail into a diagnosis or proven trigger.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of MigraLoom · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerWhat should a migraine symptom tracker record?

Record the symptom, approximate time, duration, severity or impact, and whether it was captured during the episode or recalled later.

On this page
Symptom categoriesAcross the episodeNeutral wordingSources

Important: Seek urgent medical help for a sudden extremely painful headache, a headache after serious injury, or headache with new weakness, confusion, seizure, vision loss, fever with stiff neck or other emergency signs. Follow local emergency guidance.

Useful symptom categories

Head pain

Location, quality, severity and duration.

Sensory

Light, sound, smell, vision or touch changes.

Digestive

Nausea, vomiting or appetite changes.

Cognitive

Concentration, speech or thinking changes as observed.

Function

Work, sleep, movement or usual activities affected.

Medication

What was taken, dose and time as recorded.

Track before, during and after

A symptom may begin before head pain, continue during it or remain afterward. Approximate timing is useful; false precision is not. Mark later recollections as such.

Neutral wording prevents overclaiming

Instead ofRecord
“Coffee triggered it”“Coffee at 09:00; symptoms began around 11:30”
“Medication failed”“Medication recorded at 14:20; severity still 7/10 at 15:00”
“This was aura”Describe the visual, sensory or speech change and timing

Editorial note: This guide is general information, not medical advice. It was reviewed against the sources below. A diary can support a healthcare conversation; it cannot determine a diagnosis or treatment.
