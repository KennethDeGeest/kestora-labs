Server earnings guide · United States

How much do servers make in tips?

There is no honest single tip number. The useful answer combines national wage data with the variables that change from one shift to the next.

Updated November 4, 2026 · Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and Department of Labor

The short answer

BLS reports a $16.93 median hourly wage for waiters and waitresses in the food-services industry in May 2025.

That figure includes tips. It is a national midpoint—not a promise of what a particular server, restaurant or shift will pay.

Half of workers in the occupation earn above the median and half below. Your own tips can move sharply with location, price level, section, shift length, sales, staffing, tip-out and season.

What changes a server’s tip earnings?

  • Sales and check sizeHigher sales can create more tip opportunity, but do not guarantee a higher net result.
  • Tip-outThe amount shared with bartenders, bussers or support staff reduces what you retain.
  • Hours and side workOpening, closing and slow periods change true hourly earnings.

Restaurant and shift mix

A busy fine-dining dinner and a quiet weekday lunch can produce different gross tips, but the better shift is the one that leaves stronger net earnings for the time worked.

Location and local wage law

Direct wages and tip-credit rules vary by state and locality. Federal rules provide a floor, while more protective state rules may apply.

Calculate what your shift paid

true hourly earnings = (gross tips − tip-out + wages) ÷ hours worked

Use the free tip-out calculator to combine the full shift. For example, $240 in gross tips, $36 in tip-out and $90 in wages over six hours equals $294 total, or $49 per hour before taxes.

One shift is not an average

Track at least several weeks before deciding whether a job, weekday or role is consistently better. Compare like with like: net tips, full clocked hours and wages—not only the best cash night.
