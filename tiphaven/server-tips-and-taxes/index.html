Server tips and taxes: start with a daily record

Tips are income. Keeping the shift-level details makes employer reporting and tax-time questions much easier to reconcile.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of TipHaven · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerAre server tips taxable income?

The IRS says all tips received by employees are income. They are subject to federal income tax and generally to Social Security and Medicare taxes.

The federal basics

  • Tips received directly from customers are income.
  • Distributed tips from a pool or sharing arrangement are income.
  • Noncash tips are income but follow different employer-reporting treatment.
  • Daily records support monthly employer reports and the annual return.

What the IRS treats as cash tips

Cash tips include cash received directly, electronically paid tips distributed by the employer and tips received through a pool or sharing arrangement. The label “cash tips” includes more than paper currency.

What to keep in a daily tip record

Date and workplace

Identify the shift and employer.

Cash and card tips

Record tips received before sharing.

Tips paid out

Keep recipients or categories when useful.

Pool distributions

Record amounts received from others.

Hours and wages

Useful for reconciling earnings, though not a substitute for payroll records.

Reporting tips to your employer

The IRS generally requires employees who receive $20 or more in cash tips in a calendar month from one employer to report them to that employer by the 10th day of the following month. Check current IRS instructions and exceptions.

Reporting tips on your federal return

All tip income must be included, including amounts that were not reported to the employer. Use current IRS forms and instructions or qualified help for your situation.
