Splitting tips by hours worked, step by step

An hours-based split gives every eligible worked hour the same value, then multiplies that rate by each person’s time.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of TipHaven · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerHow do you split tips by hours worked?

Add all eligible hours, divide the distributable tip pool by that total, then multiply the hourly pool rate by each person’s hours. Adjust final cents so the distributed total matches the pool exactly.

The four-step method

  • Subtract any documented deduction that applies before distribution.
  • Add every eligible person’s hours.
  • Divide distributable tips by total eligible hours.
  • Multiply that hourly pool rate by each person’s hours.

Worked example

PersonHoursShare of a $480 pool
Alex8$213.33
Jordan6$160.00
Sam4$106.67
Total18$480.00

The unrounded hourly pool rate is $26.666…; final cents must be assigned deliberately.

Handle the final cents deliberately

Calculate with full precision first, then round currency to two decimals. Assign any remaining cent difference consistently—for example, to the largest unrounded remainders—so the final allocation equals the pool.

Equal split versus hours worked

MethodBest whenTradeoff
Equal splitEvery eligible person should receive the same amountIgnores different shift lengths
Hours workedEvery eligible hour should carry equal valueRequires accurate eligible-hour records
Points or rolesResponsibilities have agreed weightsNeeds a clear documented policy

The formula does not decide eligibility

The workplace policy and applicable law determine who participates and which hours count. Under U.S. federal rules, managers and supervisors generally may not receive employees’ pooled tips. State rules may be more protective.
