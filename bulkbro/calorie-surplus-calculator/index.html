How this bulking calorie calculator works

The calculator first estimates resting energy expenditure with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, published in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. It then applies the activity factor you select to create a rough maintenance estimate and adds your chosen daily surplus.

The result is an estimate—not a direct measurement of your metabolism. Activity categories are broad, food logs contain estimation error, and individual response varies.

What your result means

Use the number as a starting target.

The useful test is whether your average intake and body-weight response move together in the direction you intended.

Resting needs

An equation-based estimate of the energy your body would use at rest. This is not your daily eating target.

Estimated maintenance

Resting needs multiplied by the selected activity level. Treat this as a rough baseline.

Starting surplus

The extra calories added above estimated maintenance. The NHS suggests that adults seeking gradual weight gain could try adding around 300–500 calories per day, while also emphasizing balanced food choices.

Why the target may need to change

A formula cannot see how your actual body weight responds. BulkBro starts with a target, then connects completed daily logs with your weekly weight response. When the observed result differs from the intended pace, the app can make a measured adjustment and record why.

That feedback loop is the key difference between calculating a number once and managing a real weight-gain plan.
