How to track calories for weight gain.

Track enough detail to compare your completed intake with your target. The goal is not a perfect diary. It is a consistent record that shows whether the plan you intended is the plan you actually followed.

Quick answer

Log food and drinks close to when you consume them, include portions, review estimates before saving, and complete every day you want included in the weekly average. Consistency matters more than false precision.

Use one repeatable logging workflow

  1. Choose a starting calorie target.
  2. Record meals, snacks and drinks during the day.
  3. Check the estimated food and portion before saving.
  4. Finish the day only when the log reflects the full day.
  5. Compare the completed week with the weight trend.

BulkBro lets you begin with a photo, spoken description or typed entry. Each route produces an editable estimate. The review step matters because no input method can know the exact ingredients or portion without your confirmation.

Treat calorie numbers as estimates

Dietary records depend on food-composition data and self-reported portions. A systematic review of dietary-record apps found that the included apps underestimated energy intake compared with reference methods, with considerable variation among studies. Image-based methods can reduce effort, but research still finds challenges when estimating intake in free-living conditions.

This does not make tracking useless. It means a consistent method is more informative than switching between unrelated assumptions. Save a common meal the same way, use package labels when available, and correct an estimate that clearly does not match what you ate.

Missing items change the question

If breakfast and lunch are logged but dinner is missing, the total does not describe the day. Drinks, cooking oils, sauces, snacks and second portions are common gaps. Do not use an incomplete number to decide that your calorie target failed.

A practical standard

Complete enough of the week that you can explain what the average represents. Mark uncertainty honestly instead of manufacturing precision.

Use averages to reduce daily noise

Compare average completed intake with the intended target. One low day can be balanced by another day; the average shows the pattern. Then compare that pattern with repeated weigh-ins under similar conditions.

If the average is below target, first solve execution. If intake matches target but weight is not moving as intended, follow the weight-gain troubleshooting checklist.

Review the log before changing the plan

  • Were all seven days complete?
  • Did portions use similar assumptions?
  • Were drinks and calorie-dense extras included?
  • Did activity change meaningfully?
  • Were weigh-ins taken under comparable conditions?

A calorie target becomes useful when the next decision can be traced to the completed evidence. That is the loop BulkBro is designed to preserve.
