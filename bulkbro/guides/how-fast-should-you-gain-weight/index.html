Weight-gain pace · Practical guide

How fast should you gain weight?

For muscle-focused gain, a useful published starting range for novice and intermediate natural bodybuilders is about 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week. It is a reference point, not a promise. Training experience, starting body composition and your real response should shape the pace.

Quick answer

Pick a modest weekly pace, measure under consistent conditions, and judge several observations together. A faster scale increase does not guarantee faster muscle growth and can increase fat gain.

Choose the rate before the calories

A weight-gain target is easier to evaluate when it includes a pace. A narrative review of off-season nutrition for natural bodybuilders proposed roughly 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week for novice and intermediate lifters, with more advanced lifters using a more conservative approach. That range is specific to a muscle-focused training context and should not be treated as a universal medical recommendation.

For general healthy weight gain, the NHS advises gradual gain and suggests that adults could try adding around 300–500 calories per day. These are two different ways of framing a starting plan: one describes a weekly outcome, while the other describes an intake change.

What 0.25–0.5% looks like

Body weight0.25% per week0.5% per week
60 kg0.15 kg0.30 kg
75 kg0.19 kg0.38 kg
90 kg0.23 kg0.45 kg

These calculations show the scale of the range. They do not predict how much of a change will be muscle, fat, fluid or food mass.

Use a trend, not a deadline

Body weight can move from day to day even when the underlying direction has not changed. Use the same scale at a similar time and compare repeated readings. A single result above or below the target pace is not enough to diagnose the plan.

It also matters whether you completed the intake target. A slow trend after averaging below the target means something different from a slow trend after a complete, consistently logged week. Read how to track calories for weight gain before interpreting incomplete data.

Change one input at a time

A 2023 study in resistance-trained participants found that faster body-mass gain was more strongly associated with increased skinfold thickness than with better strength or muscle-thickness outcomes. The study was small, but it supports a cautious principle: a much larger surplus is not automatically a better muscle-building plan.

If the trend remains below your intended pace after consistent logging and measurement, make a measured calorie adjustment. If it is above the range, consider reducing the surplus rather than waiting for a long correction phase. Keep the goal in health context

A bodybuilding rate is not appropriate for every person. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional if your weight is low, falling unintentionally, affected by a medical condition or medication, or connected to disordered eating. People under 18 and those who are pregnant or breastfeeding need individual guidance.
