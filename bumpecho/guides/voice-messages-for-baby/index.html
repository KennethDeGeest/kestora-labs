A voice message can carry a whole family

Invite the people you love to record one small, specific welcome. Their natural voices are the point—no script, microphone or polished performance required.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Bump Echo · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerWhat should you say in a voice message to a future baby?

Introduce yourself, explain how you are connected to the family, share one short story and end with a simple welcome. Thirty to ninety seconds is plenty.

Prompts for the people waiting

Parent or caregiver

What changed when you learned about the pregnancy? What ordinary day do you want to remember?

Grandparent

Which family story or tradition would you like to pass forward?

Sibling

What are you excited to show, teach or do together?

Friend

What do you hope this child eventually understands about their family?

How to keep the message natural

  • Record somewhere quiet enough to hear, but not so formal that everyone freezes.
  • Give the person one prompt instead of a full script.
  • Keep the laughter, pause or second attempt if it feels like them.
  • Add their name, relationship and the date in the title or note.

Ask before keeping and sharing

Tell each person what the recording is for and who may hear it. A family keepsake is still someone else’s voice. Do not publish or broadly share it without clear permission.
