How do you start a letter to an unborn baby?

Begin with a concrete present-tense detail: where you are, how far along the pregnancy is or what happened today. Specific details make the letter feel real and remove the pressure to sound profound.

A gentle three-part structure

  1. Here is where we are. Describe today in a few honest details.
  2. Here is what I hope. Share a value, feeling or wish without deciding who the child must become.
  3. Here is your welcome. End with the words you want them to hear from you.

Prompts that leave room for your story

TodayThe weather is… The room sounds like… The person beside me just said…
This seasonI keep thinking about… I did not expect… One small thing that changed me is…
Our peopleSomeone waiting to meet you is… A story from our family I want you to know is…
For laterIf you ever hear this when… I hope you remember… You never have to be…

Read the letter aloud

The written letter keeps the exact words. A recording keeps how you said them. Read naturally, include the stumbled sentence and do not chase a studio-quality result. The recognizable voice is the keepsake.
