What belongs in a pregnancy memory book?

Start with a timeline, then add small personal details: the day you found out, family reactions, appointment-day thoughts, name ideas, photos of ordinary life, letters and voice recordings. Meaning matters more than completeness.

Six sections that create a real story

The beginningHow you found out, where you were and what the next hour felt like.
The peopleReactions, family stories, messages and the names of people waiting with you.
The everydayMeals, songs, walks, rooms and routines that defined this particular season.
The changesWhat shifted in your body, plans, relationship or view of the future.
The preparationLists, little purchases, the nursery or the bag by the door.
The voicesA first hello, a story, a song or a message meant to be replayed later.

Use each medium for what it remembers best

A photo captures appearance. A caption supplies context. A longer entry records thought. Audio keeps timing, accent, hesitation and laughter. A physical object adds touch. You do not need every format for every milestone—choose the one that carries the most meaning.

How to avoid an unfinished memory book

  • Create the sections first and fill them out of order.
  • Let blank spaces remain blank instead of inventing a perfect narrative.
  • Keep a small “inbox” of photos, notes and recordings to organize later.
  • Choose one monthly moment to curate, not an entire month to reconstruct.

If you prefer a lighter system, compare a pregnancy audio journal with a traditional book.
