A pregnancy journal made of real moments

A useful pregnancy journal does not need a perfect daily entry. It needs small, honest pieces of the story—written, photographed or heard in the voices you love.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Bump Echo · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerWhat should you put in a pregnancy journal?

Record the things your future self may want back: ordinary routines, questions, changing hopes, family reactions and the voices around you. A short weekly entry is often enough.

Start with a rhythm you can keep

A blank book can make every entry feel important. That pressure is usually the enemy. Pick one small ritual—Sunday evening, after an appointment or whenever a new week begins—and capture one thing that changed, one thing you noticed and one thing you want to remember.

  • Keep entries small. A sentence, photo caption or 20-second voice note counts.
  • Date the moment. Context becomes surprisingly valuable later.
  • Write what is true today. You do not need to make every entry cheerful or polished.
  • Mix formats. Text remembers facts; audio remembers pace, laughter and tone.

Pregnancy journal prompts by trimester

First trimester

How did you find out? Who knew first? What are you quietly hoping for?

Second trimester

What changes feel real now? Which name, song or family story keeps returning?

Third trimester

What are you preparing? What do you want to remember about waiting?

After birth

What surprised you? Which pregnancy details already feel far away?

Why add audio to a pregnancy journal?

Written entries preserve meaning. Audio preserves presence. A grandparent’s greeting, a partner reading a story or your own unedited thoughts can carry details that a transcript cannot. The goal is not a performance; it is a recognizable piece of a real day.
