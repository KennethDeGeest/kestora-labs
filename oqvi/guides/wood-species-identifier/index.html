Wood identification begins at the end grain

Color and face grain are memorable but variable. A clean transverse surface can reveal pores, rings, rays and resin canals that carry stronger evidence.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Oqvi · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerWhat photo is most useful for wood identification?

Photograph a clean end-grain surface plus face and edge grain. Record source, region, odor and density separately. A photo cannot certify species, grade, toxicity or suitability.

Three surfaces reveal different clues

End grain

Pores, growth rings, rays, parenchyma and resin canals.

Face grain

Figure, color variation and longitudinal vessel pattern.

Edge grain

Ring orientation and another longitudinal view.

Prepare a useful photograph

  • Make a clean, flat transverse cut when safe and appropriate.
  • Use sharp focus and even light.
  • Include a ruler or scale.
  • Photograph all three surfaces.
  • Record source, region, odor, weight and finishing history separately.

Why an app cannot certify a wood species

Finish, age, lighting, stain and weathering change appearance. Closely related woods may require microscopic anatomy, chemical testing or provenance information. Photo matching is a candidate-generating step.

Use a specialist when the answer matters

Seek professional identification for structural work, protected species, customs, high-value trade, restoration, toxicity, burning or food-contact decisions.
