What to compare before installing

  1. ScopeDoes it recognize only living plants, or also bark, timber, leaves and whole-tree context?
  2. EvidenceCan you add several views and see alternatives instead of treating one photo as certainty?
  3. MemoryCan you keep notes, favorites and places after the identification?
  4. PrivacyDoes your saved collection remain local, join a public community, or require an account?
  5. PriceCheck the current App Store screen for subscriptions, trials and free-use limits before committing.

When Oqvi is the right fit

Choose Oqvi when the find matters beyond the first answer: a tree on a regular walk, a flower you want to remember, or a piece of timber whose grain caught your attention. Saved discoveries, notes and precise map locations live in the app’s local storage on your iPhone. Photos and questions you deliberately submit are processed online.

Choose Pl@ntNet when free, plant-specific citizen science is central. Choose Seek when you want a family-friendly route into many kinds of wildlife. Use Apple Visual Look Up when you want a quick result without building a collection.

Treat every result as a suggestion

No photo identifier should be the sole basis for deciding whether a plant is edible, toxic or medicinal, or whether wood is safe for structural or fire-related use. Photograph multiple clues, compare the result with trusted regional references and consult a qualified expert when the consequence of being wrong matters.

FIELD SAFETY

Identification results can be incomplete or wrong. Never rely on a photo or AI answer for edibility, toxicity, medical care, fire safety, structural use or another consequential decision. Confirm with a qualified expert.
