Oqvi field guide

Your iPhone can name a plant. A field app helps you remember it

Apple already offers a useful photo lookup. Dedicated apps become valuable when you want more evidence, questions, notes and a collection.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Oqvi · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerDo you need a separate plant identifier app?

Start with the tool already on your iPhone. Add a dedicated app when you need repeated discoveries, several botanical materials, notes, favorites, questions or optional places.

Try Apple Visual Look Up first

  • Open a supported photo in Photos.
  • Look for the information button with visual stars.
  • Tap the identified subject to review suggestions.
  • Remember that availability varies by region, language, device and photo.

When a dedicated plant identifier earns its place

Multiple views

Keep the whole plant and detail photos together.

Field notes

Record habitat, season and uncertainty.

Collection

Return to discoveries instead of losing them in Photos.

Follow-up

Ask about a feature or compare another material.

Take a better identification set

Capture the whole subject, attachment details and reproductive structures when present. Avoid relying on one dramatic close-up.

Read the privacy model, not just the feature list

Check whether an account is required, what is uploaded for processing, whether places are optional and how saved discoveries can be deleted or exported.

Editorial note: This field guide is educational information. Photo identification produces candidates, not certainty. The article was reviewed against the sources below; use an appropriate regional reference or qualified expert when the answer matters.
