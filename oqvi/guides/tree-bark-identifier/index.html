Bark is a pattern, not a paint sample

Color alone shifts with light, moisture and age. Photograph the repeating structure, the whole trunk and any leaves, buds or fruit you can safely reach.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Oqvi · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerCan bark identify a tree?

Bark is strongest as one clue in a full tree record. Capture a sharp close-up, a wider repeating pattern and the full trunk, then add leaves, buds, fruit or crown shape.

Five bark features worth naming

Texture

Smooth, flaky, fibrous, scaly, corky or deeply furrowed.

Pattern

Ridges, plates, diamonds, strips or horizontal marks.

Depth

Shallow lines or deep furrows and raised ridges.

Color range

Describe several tones rather than one paint-chip color.

Age change

Young branch, middle trunk and mature base may differ.

Photograph bark for identification

  • Take a square close-up in even light.
  • Step back to show the repeating pattern.
  • Photograph the full trunk and branching form.
  • Add leaves, buds, fruit or fallen material without damaging the tree.
  • Record region, habitat and whether it appears planted.

Why bark-only identification is difficult

Bark changes with age, damage, moisture, light and growing conditions. Different species can converge on similar patterns, while one species can look different across the trunk.

Keep the bark with the tree

Store bark views beside the whole tree and seasonal clues. A later leaf or fruit photograph may resolve what a winter bark image could not.
