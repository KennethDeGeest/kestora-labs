A tree is more than a single leaf

The strongest identification combines silhouette, branching, leaves, bark, buds, fruit and location—not one overloaded photo.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Oqvi · Updated November 11, 2026 · 1 minute read

Quick answerWhat should you photograph for a tree identification app?

Photograph the whole tree and at least two close details. Leaves are an easy starting point, while bark, buds, fruit, branch arrangement and seasonal context separate lookalikes.

A six-photo tree record

Whole tree

Crown, height and branching habit.

Twig

Opposite, alternate or whorled attachment.

Leaf

Upper and lower surface with scale.

Bark

Close texture and wider repeating pattern.

Seasonal clue

Bud, flower, fruit, cone or seed.

Habitat

Ground, neighboring vegetation and growing context.

Why one clue is rarely enough

Leaf shape can overlap, bark changes with age and fruit may be absent. Independent clues reduce the chance that one misleading feature controls the result.

Build one discovery from several views

Keep every view, field note and candidate inside one record. Add another season later instead of creating a disconnected identification.

When to seek confirmation

Use a regional key or qualified expert for management, pruning, disease, hazards, conservation, legality, food or poisoning questions.

Editorial note: This field guide is educational information. Photo identification produces candidates, not certainty. The article was reviewed against the sources below; use an appropriate regional reference or qualified expert when the answer matters.
