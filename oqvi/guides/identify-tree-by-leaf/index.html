Read the leaf from the twig outward

Shape gets attention, but attachment and arrangement often reveal more. Keep the twig in frame before moving closer to the blade.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Oqvi · Updated November 11, 2026 · 1 minute read

Quick answerWhich leaf features identify a tree?

Begin with simple versus compound and opposite versus alternate arrangement. Then record margin, lobes, veins, texture, tip and base before confirming with bark, buds, fruit and tree form.

A practical leaf key for beginners

Attachment

Simple blade or compound leaf with leaflets?

Arrangement

Opposite, alternate or whorled on the twig?

Margin

Smooth, toothed, double-toothed or lobed?

Venation

Surface

Hairy, waxy, leathery, rough or soft?

Shape

Tip, base, symmetry and overall outline.

Three photos that preserve the evidence

  • Twig view showing several attachments.
  • Flat upper and lower leaf surfaces.
  • Whole tree, bark or seasonal structure for context.

Do not stop when leaves fall

Winter buds, twig color, leaf scars, bark, fruit and branching habit can identify a tree when leaves are absent or damaged.

Confirm the candidate with independent clues

A leaf suggests a candidate; agreement across bark, buds, fruit, range and tree form makes it stronger. Use a regional key and keep uncertain records labelled as such.

Editorial note: This field guide is educational information. Photo identification produces candidates, not certainty. The article was reviewed against the sources below; use an appropriate regional reference or qualified expert when the answer matters.
