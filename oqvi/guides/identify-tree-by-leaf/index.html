Leaf key

Read the leaf from the twig outward.

Shape gets attention, but attachment and arrangement often reveal more. Keep the twig in frame before moving closer to the blade.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Oqvi · Updated November 5, 2026

Begin with simple versus compound, then check opposite versus alternate arrangement.

After that, record the margin, lobes, veins, texture, tip, base and seasonal color. Confirm with bark, buds, fruit and tree form.

A practical leaf key for beginners

  1. Simple or compound?A simple leaf has one blade; a compound leaf divides into leaflets. Find the bud at the base of the full leaf stalk.
  2. Opposite or alternate?Look at where leaves attach along the twig, not how they overlap in the photo.
  3. Needle, scale or broad leaf?For conifers, note needle bundles, length and how they attach.
  4. MarginRecord whether the edge is smooth, toothed, double-toothed or lobed.
  5. Veins and textureLook for pinnate or palmate venation, gloss, hairs and color beneath.
  6. Base and tipThese small shapes can distinguish otherwise similar leaves.

Three photos that preserve the evidence

Photograph the leaf while it is still attached to a twig, then the upper surface and the underside. Use even natural light and keep the edge sharp. Add a scale reference only when it is safe and will not cover important features.

Do not stop when leaves fall

Deciduous trees lose the easiest clue. Winter identification shifts toward buds, twig color, leaf scars, bark, persistent fruit and overall form. A saved summer record makes those winter comparisons more useful.

Confirm the candidate

Penn State notes that leaf keys are a common starting point but keys also exist for bark, buds, fruit and cones. Compare several organs and use a regional key because the likely species changes with geography.

FIELD SAFETY

Identification results can be incomplete or wrong. Never rely on a photo or AI answer for edibility, toxicity, medical care, fire safety, structural use or another consequential decision. Confirm with a qualified expert.
