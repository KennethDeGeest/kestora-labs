Plant identification

Name the clues before you name the plant.

Identification improves when you record what is actually present—growth habit, leaf arrangement, stem, flower, fruit and habitat—before accepting a suggestion.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Oqvi · Updated November 5, 2026

PLANT RECORD / 10Several organs, one findUncertainty is useful

Field index

Quick answerOqvi in the fieldSourcesRelated guides
Photograph the whole plant, leaf attachment, both leaf surfaces and any flower or fruit.

Record region, habitat, season and whether it appears cultivated. Compare several independent features before settling on a name.

Observe from large to small

  1. Growth habitTree, shrub, vine, grasslike plant, rosette or upright herb.
  2. StemWoody or soft; round, square, ridged, hairy, thorny or hollow.
  3. Leaf arrangementOpposite, alternate, whorled or basal; simple or compound.
  4. Leaf detailsShape, margin, veins, texture, hairs, color beneath and attachment.
  5. Flowers and fruitCount parts and record symmetry, clusters, seed structures and season.
  6. HabitatRegion, moisture, light, soil, elevation and whether the plant is cultivated.

Build a useful photo set

Take one image wide enough to show the full growth habit, then move closer for the leaf on its stem, the underside, the flower from two angles and any fruit. Keep photos from the same individual together. Avoid pulling or damaging a plant merely for identification.

Compare, do not merely match

A candidate should explain several observed features at once. Check a regional flora or trusted institutional source and actively look for reasons the suggestion might be wrong. Similar-looking plants may differ in a small but important structure.

Save a field record, not just a result

Oqvi keeps the submitted views, suggested name, follow-up context, notes and optional location together. That record is useful even when the first answer remains uncertain because you can add a new seasonal clue later.

Never use a photo result as a safety decision

Do not eat, touch, burn, prepare or medically use an unknown plant based only on an app. For toxicity, allergies, invasive-species management or treatment decisions, consult the appropriate qualified local professional.

FIELD SAFETY

Identification results can be incomplete or wrong. Never rely on a photo or AI answer for edibility, toxicity, medical care, fire safety, structural use or another consequential decision. Confirm with a qualified expert.
