Name the clues before you name the plant

Identification improves when you record what is actually present before accepting a suggestion.

Published by Kestora Labs, the maker of Oqvi · Updated November 11, 2026 · 2 minute read

Quick answerWhat photos help identify a plant?

Photograph the whole plant, leaf attachment, both leaf surfaces and any flower or fruit. Record region, habitat, season and whether it appears cultivated.

Important: Never eat, touch, treat, burn or give a plant to a person or animal based only on a photo-app result. Use a qualified local expert or poison service when safety is involved.

Observe from large to small

Growth habit

Tree, shrub, vine, grass-like plant or herb?

Leaves

Arrangement, shape, margin, veins, texture and attachment.

Stem

Round or square, smooth or hairy, woody or soft.

Flowers and fruit

Number of petals, clusters, color, structure and maturity.

Context

Region, habitat, season and cultivated or wild setting.

Build a useful photo set

  • Photograph the whole plant in its setting.
  • Show how leaves attach to the stem.
  • Capture upper and lower leaf surfaces.
  • Add a sharp flower or fruit view.
  • Include a size reference without damaging the plant.

Compare, do not merely match

Use the app result as a candidate. Compare several independent features with a regional flora, key or trusted reference. Look actively for differences, not only similarities.

Save a field record, not just a result

Keep the photos, date, habitat, observed features and uncertainty beside the candidate name. That makes the discovery useful even when the identification changes later.

Editorial note: This field guide is educational information. Photo identification produces candidates, not certainty. The article was reviewed against the sources below; use an appropriate regional reference or qualified expert when the answer matters.
